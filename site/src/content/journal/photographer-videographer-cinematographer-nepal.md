Families often ask us the same thing in the first call: do we need a photographer, a videographer or a cinematographer? The honest answer is that most Nepali weddings need photos and film together. But the words mean different things, and knowing the difference helps you book the right team.

## What is the difference between a photographer, a videographer and a cinematographer?

A **photographer** makes still pictures. These are the portraits on the wall, the album pages and the photos your relatives ask for on WhatsApp the next morning.

A **videographer** records the day as it happens. The aim is coverage: the rituals, the speeches, the dancing, so nothing is missed.

A **cinematographer** also shoots video, but with a story in mind. Light, framing, sound and music matter. The result feels more like a short film than a recording.

In practice, a good wedding film team does both. They record the important moments in full, and they shoot the beautiful moments with care.

## What does each one cover across the rituals?

A Nepali wedding is not one event. It is several days of rituals, and each one asks for something different.

- **Mehendi and haldi:** close, colourful, full of laughter. Photos catch the hands and faces. Film catches the songs and the turmeric fights.
- **Janti:** the groom's procession arrives with music and dancing. Video carries the energy here better than stills.
- **Kanyadan:** a quiet, emotional moment. The photographer needs to know where to stand and when to stay still.
- **Sindoor pote:** fast and easy to miss. Both photo and film should be ready before it starts.
- **Bidaai:** the farewell. Tears, hugs, the last look back. Film often holds this moment best.
- **Reception:** portraits with family, the stage, the speeches and the party.

The common thread is ritual knowledge. Someone who knows what comes next can be in the right place without asking the priest to wait.

## Reels, highlight film, full film or documentary film: which do you need?

These are the four kinds of wedding video you will hear about.

- **Instagram reels:** short, vertical clips for social media. Fun to share, quick to watch.
- **Cinematic highlight film:** usually 4 to 6 minutes, cut to music. This is the film most couples watch again and again.
- **Full wedding film:** a longer edit that keeps the rituals and speeches in order. This is the one your parents and grandparents will want.
- **Documentary film:** a longer, story-led film of 10 to 15 minutes. It is part of our Heritage package, along with a same-day edit reel.

Many families choose a highlight film for themselves and a full film for the family archive.

## How many photographers and videographers does a wedding need?

This depends on how many sides you want covered and how big the day is.

- **Bride side or groom side only:** one photographer and one videographer can usually cover a single family's events well.
- **Both families:** two homes, two sets of rituals, and often two places at once. This is when a second photographer or videographer helps, so neither side is missed.
- **Large weddings:** for big guest lists or a multi-day schedule, two photographers and two cinematographers at every event gives full coverage. That is how our Heritage weddings are staffed.

If you are unsure, tell us your guest count and schedule. We will suggest the smallest team that does the job properly.

## How do you choose a wedding photo studio in Nepal?

Before you book, ask these questions:

1. **Do you know our rituals?** Ask them to walk you through a Hindu or Newari wedding. Listen for kanyadan, sindoor pote and bidaai.
2. **Can I see full galleries, not just highlights?** Anyone can show twenty good photos. A full wedding gallery shows how they handle a crowded room and bad light.
3. **When will we get a sneak peek?** You will want something to share soon after the wedding.
4. **When will the final photos and films be delivered?** Ask for the dates in writing.
5. **Is there a contract?** It should list the events, hours, team size, prices and delivery dates.
